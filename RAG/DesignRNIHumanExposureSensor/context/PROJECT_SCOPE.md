
## ✔ Project Title

**SDR-Based Monitoring of IoT Exposure in High-Density Device Environments**

---

## ✔ Problem Area – Justification

The importance of monitoring electric field radiation (electric field exposure) in work environments is highlighted due to its potential impact on health. This study focuses on radiation levels in enclosed spaces with a high concentration of IoT devices and their effect on worker health [1]. The proliferation of these technologies in places such as offices and factories has raised concerns about the hazards of exposure to electric fields generated by these devices. ITU-T Recommendation K.52 provides guidelines and assessment methods to ensure that telecommunications facilities comply with safety limits, based on the criteria of the International Commission on Non-Ionizing Radiation Protection (ICNIRP).

Traditional spectrum analyzers present several important challenges. They are bulky and expensive devices, which hinders their portability and economic accessibility [2]. This limitation is particularly problematic for propagation studies in urban areas. In addition, the need to spectrally monitor IoT networks requires advanced techniques such as spectrum sensing, which are not always available or compatible with traditional equipment [3]. An alternative is the use of software-defined radio (SDR) due to its practicality and low cost. However, SDRs face limitations such as a limited dynamic range, challenges in implementing analog-digital interfaces, and restrictions on the maximum frequency usable by the analog-to-digital converter [4][5][6]. The implementation of spectrum monitoring systems faces several significant challenges. First, an adaptable design optimized for IoT applications is needed, capable of operating over a wide frequency range, from 700 MHz to 3.5 GHz [7]. Without this capability, assessments cannot cover the most critical operating bands. Furthermore, in urban environments, speed and precision in performing hundreds of measurements are essential to identify areas with high radiation levels and ensure compliance with current regulations. The lack of instruments able to meet these demands results in reduced monitoring efficiency, as well as a considerable increase in the cost and time required to carry out traditional measurement campaigns [8].

To optimize the monitoring and control of electric field radiation in enclosed areas with a high concentration of IoT devices, it is essential to develop advanced thresholding algorithms that integrate machine learning techniques [9]. These algorithms make it possible to dynamically adapt detection thresholds in response to fluctuations and the complexity of signals and noise, extracting useful patterns from historical and current data [10]. However, common methods — such as spectral sensing based on covariance or eigenvalues for thresholding — often face difficulties such as threshold selection under low signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) conditions [11][10], and the need for frequent adjustments due to the rapid frequency changes caused by the diversity of IoT devices operating across different bands, which complicates the dynamic adaptability of the algorithms [12]. This complexity stems from variations in attenuation and from the simultaneous emissions of devices with variable transmission parameters and power [13]. Furthermore, the dependence of adaptive methods on specific parameters can complicate calibration and affect system effectiveness in dynamic scenarios [14]. The lack of advanced algorithms capable of managing these variations, combined with the limited capacity of current systems to integrate data from multiple sources [15], means detection can be inaccurate and generate a high number of false alarms [2]. The inefficiency and lack of precision in monitoring radiation in densely device-equipped zones compromises both safety and operational efficiency [16].

Finally, the generation of electric field maps presents several important problems that must be considered. First, accurately correlating IoT device density with the radiation levels they emit is a challenge due to environmental variability and the complexity of the emitted signals [3]. In addition, measuring device concentration in enclosed spaces can be difficult to perform accurately, which complicates the evaluation of observed radiation levels [17]. Another significant problem is interpreting data in relation to the frequencies used and their possible adverse health effects, since this requires deep and up-to-date knowledge of scientific and medical studies in this area [18].

It is essential to carry out thorough and precise evaluations so as not to underestimate the risks associated with prolonged exposure to electric fields, especially in work environments with continuous exposure [18]. The effects of prolonged exposure may include neurological alterations and sleep disorders, and in more severe cases, an increased risk of degenerative or cancerous conditions [19].

In addition, it is crucial to address challenges such as internal space-charge accumulation and charge perturbations that alter the distribution of the electric field [20]. These problems are significant in environments with high radiation density and can lead to incorrect assessments of compliance with safety standards [18].

---

## ✔ Research Question

How does the density of IoT devices influence electric field exposure levels in enclosed environments, and what impact does this exposure have on worker health, using a monitoring system based on Software-Defined Radio (SDR) and advanced machine learning techniques for the precise detection and analysis of these radiations?

---

## ✔ Objectives: General and Specific

### GENERAL OBJECTIVE

To develop a system for evaluating human exposure levels to electric fields, based on software-defined radio (SDR) and machine learning techniques, that enables measurement of radiation density in enclosed environments with high IoT device occupancy.

### SPECIFIC OBJECTIVES

1. Design and build an SDR-based measurement module that enables spectral monitoring of IoT networks with high device density in enclosed spaces, employing spectral sensing techniques.

2. Develop thresholding algorithms and cooperative processes based on machine learning methods to handle signal measurement variability in enclosed environments with a high interference index.

3. Generate spatial electric field maps using advanced measurement and data analysis techniques to assess compliance with human exposure regulations, providing results through detailed reports and technical presentations to regulatory bodies and interested organizations.

### Regulation of the Radioelectric Spectrum in Colombia

In Colombia, the regulation of the radioelectric spectrum is administered by the National Spectrum Agency (Agencia Nacional del Espectro — ANE), an entity responsible for planning, coordinating, and controlling spectrum use. The ANE is responsible for ensuring efficient and equitable spectrum distribution, preventing and resolving interference, and complying with international regulations. Regulation includes license assignment, oversight of compliance with technical and legal standards, and the implementation of policies to promote efficient spectrum use [33].

### HackRF One Demodulator

The HackRF One is a versatile and powerful device used in software-defined radio (SDR) applications. This device is capable of receiving and transmitting signals across a wide frequency range, from 1 MHz to 6 GHz, making it suitable for a variety of RF applications [34]. Its open, flexible design allows users to experiment with RF and develop their own communication systems.

**Characteristics of the HackRF One Demodulator:** The HackRF One stands out for its wide operating frequency range, high sensitivity, and bidirectional transmission capability. It can be used both to receive and to transmit signals, giving it great flexibility. It is also compatible with a wide range of SDR software, facilitating its integration into different projects and applications. Its open design and the active community that supports it provide a vast resource for learning and experimentation in the field of SDR.

**Applications of the HackRF One Demodulator:** Applications of the HackRF One range from research and development in wireless communications to teaching and amateur radio hobbyism. Professionally, it is used for spectrum analysis, communication system testing, and new protocol development [35]. In education, it serves as a practical tool for teaching RF and SDR concepts. It is also popular among radio amateurs for exploring a wide range of frequencies and experimenting with different transmission modes.

### Jetson Nano

The Jetson Nano is a small, powerful computing platform designed by NVIDIA, oriented primarily toward artificial intelligence (AI) applications and machine learning on edge devices. This system-on-chip (SoC) includes a CPU, a GPU, and a series of other components necessary to perform intensive processing tasks in a compact, energy-efficient format. The Jetson Nano is popular in robotics, IoT (Internet of Things), and AI projects due to its ability to run deep learning models in real time and its accessibility to developers and hobbyists [36].

**Technical Overview of the Jetson Nano:** NVIDIA's Jetson Nano is a development platform featuring a Quad-core ARM Cortex-A57 CPU and an NVIDIA Maxwell GPU with 128 CUDA cores. It generally comes with 4 GB of LPDDR4 RAM and supports a range of I/O interfaces, including USB, HDMI, and GPIO, among others. Its graphics processing capability and inclusion of AI accelerators make it ideal for computer vision applications and real-time data processing. The Jetson Nano is capable of running multiple neural networks in parallel and processing data from various high-resolution sensors, making it a powerful solution for edge AI projects.

**Jetson Nano Models:** NVIDIA has released different Jetson Nano models, each geared toward different needs and applications. The best-known models are the Jetson Nano Developer Kit and the Jetson Nano 2GB Developer Kit. While the former offers 4 GB of RAM and full support for the Raspberry Pi camera, the latter is a more accessible version with 2 GB of RAM, aimed at hobbyists and educators. Both models share most technical characteristics, such as CPU and GPU, but differ in memory capacity and some connectivity features.

**Software Compatible with the Jetson Nano:** The Jetson Nano is compatible with a wide range of software, including Linux-based operating systems such as Ubuntu. It is also compatible with AI and machine learning tools and frameworks such as TensorFlow, PyTorch, CUDA, and cuDNN. NVIDIA provides the JetPack SDK, which includes the operating system, libraries, and APIs necessary for developing AI applications. Additionally, due to its popularity within the developer community, a wide variety of third-party software and open-source projects are available for the Jetson Nano.

**Jetson Nano Applications:** The Jetson Nano is used in a variety of applications that require edge AI processing. This includes robotics projects, autonomous drones, computer-vision surveillance systems, smart personal assistants, and IoT devices. Its ability to process and analyze images and video in real time makes it ideal for computer vision applications. It is also used in education and research to teach and develop AI and machine learning projects, such as precision agriculture [37].

### GNU Radio Software

GNU Radio is a free, open-source project that provides a set of tools for designing and implementing software-defined radio (SDR) systems [38]. This framework allows users to create, simulate, and deploy radio-frequency signal-processing applications without the need for specialized hardware. GNU Radio is widely used in research, education, and the development of commercial and amateur applications. It facilitates the manipulation of radio signals, from capture to decoding, and is compatible with a variety of SDR hardware devices, enabling a wide range of experimentation and development in the field of wireless communications.

**Characteristics of GNU Radio Software:** GNU Radio is characterized by its flexibility and versatility. It offers an extensive library of modular components (blocks) that can be combined to create complex signal-processing flows. These blocks include functionality for filtering, modulation/demodulation, encoding/decoding, and other signal-processing operations. GNU Radio is capable of processing both real-time signals and stored data. Its open-source nature fosters ongoing collaboration and innovation, allowing users to modify and improve the software according to their needs. In addition, its graphical interface, GNU Radio Companion, facilitates the creation and visualization of signal-processing workflows through a drag-and-drop interface.

**Integration with Neural Network Models and Deep Learning in GNU Radio:** The integration of GNU Radio with neural network models and deep learning represents a significant advancement in the field of wireless communications and signal processing. This integration allows users to apply advanced AI techniques to improve signal classification, anomaly detection, and spectrum optimization. For example, neural networks can be trained to identify specific types of radio signals or to perform complex demodulation tasks that would be difficult to achieve with traditional methods [39]. GNU Radio's ability to process signals in real time, combined with the power of neural networks and deep learning, opens new possibilities for smarter, more efficient communication systems.

### Methodology for Specific Objective 3

**Activity 3.1: Development of Sensing and Mapping Techniques**
*Description:* Development and implementation of advanced techniques for spatial sensing, specifically designed for generating field measurement maps. Using technology such as the Jetson Nano, the aim is to facilitate efficient real-time data processing. The developed techniques will enable the creation of detailed, precise visual representations of measurement fields, essential for analysis and decision-making based on environmental and electrical data.
*Tasks:*
- Research on spatial sensing methods.
- Development of algorithms for generating field maps.
- Testing and validation of sensing and mapping techniques.
*Expected results:* Developed sensing and mapping techniques.
*Means of verification:* Documentation and test results of the developed techniques.

**Activity 3.2: Generation and Evaluation of Field Measurement Maps**
*Description:* The use of interpolation and selective sampling methods will be explored to optimize the distribution of measurement points, thereby reducing the effort and cost associated with the mapping process without compromising the precision and resolution of the collected data [41]. This demonstrates the effectiveness of Kriging interpolation and selective sampling in minimizing the number of measurement points needed to generate electric field exposure maps in indoor environments.
*Tasks:*
- Data collection using the SDR module.
- Generation of field maps using the collected data.
- Evaluation of the maps against exposure standards.
*Expected results:* Generated and evaluated field maps.
*Means of verification:* Evaluation reports comparing the field maps against exposure standards.
